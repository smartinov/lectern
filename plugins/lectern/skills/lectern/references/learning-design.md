# Learning design and evidence

Reviewed 2026-09-14. The recommendations below are design choices informed by
research, not guarantees for an individual learner. Most studies are not about
self-paced technical Kindle courses; state that transfer limitation honestly.

## Retrieval and spacing

[Carpenter, Pan, and Butler (2022)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44159-022-00089-1)
review evidence supporting retrieval practice and spacing across learning
contexts, and discuss why learners often underuse them. The publisher's abstract
and research overview were available; do not imply a full-text audit of paywalled
material. [Dunlosky et al. (2013)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/1529100612453266)
rate practice testing and distributed practice highly across their reviewed
conditions. Neither establishes one universally optimal review interval.

Application: require an attempt before showing an answer; provide corrective
feedback; revisit taught objectives after a delay. Later lessons include an
earlier objective in a new context. Conversational practice prioritizes previous
errors and includes delayed recall of previously answered material. Never turn
an answer seen in the appendix into a recorded successful attempt.

## Worked examples, explanation, and transfer

The [IES practice guide (2007)](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1)
recommends spacing, alternating worked solutions with problem solving, connecting
abstract and concrete representations, quizzing, and deep explanatory questions.
Its school-oriented evidence informs this adult workflow; the audience differs.

Application: explain a completed example, reduce support in a related task, then
ask for independent application. Ask why a choice works and what would change
under a different condition. Introduce prerequisites before demanding independent
performance. Adapt support to demonstrated knowledge, rather than imposing the
same scaffolding on every topic and learner.

Use diagrams when the relationships are easier to understand spatially. Explain
them in prose, include contextual alternative text, and make them readable in
grayscale. A diagram is useful because of the content it conveys, not because a
profile labels someone a visual learner.

## Conversational writing and humor

[Mayer et al. (2004)](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ684999) found a transfer benefit,
but not a retention benefit, from conversational wording in narrated animations.
That narrow result supports trying an approachable tone; it does not prove that
long narrative prose, profanity, or any particular author's voice improves memory.

[Bender, Renkl, and Endres (2026)](https://bpspsychub.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/bjep.70080)
tested relevant and irrelevant humor in two narrated-slideshow studies. Relevant
humor outperformed irrelevant humor on transfer in one study and recall in the
other, but did not clearly outperform the plain control condition. Effects and
learner populations differed between studies.

Application: humor should explain a mechanism, make a misconception visible, or
clarify a consequence. Remove jokes and anecdotes that pull attention away from
the lesson's objective. Preserve an engaging voice without promising a memory
boost. Do not make definitions, critical distinctions, or answer rubrics depend
on interpreting irony.

## Personalization without learning-style diagnoses

[Pashler et al. (2008)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26162104/) distinguish
preferences from evidence that matching instruction to a diagnosed style improves
learning. Their review found no adequate basis for applying those assessments
generally; this is not a claim that individual differences or preferences do not
exist.

Application: personalize goals, example domains, background explanations, pace,
accessibility, and voice. Record self-reported familiarity separately from quiz
evidence. Match representations to the subject and task. Do not infer personality,
diagnoses, or a fixed learning type from mistakes or writing preferences.

## Lesson and review checklist

- One central question; two or three observable outcomes where the scope allows.
- A few coherent explanatory chunks, supported examples, independent application,
  and a recap connected to the opening problem.
- Usually five self-check questions: explanation/recall, application/misconceptions,
  and transfer. Adjust the count if answering them exceeds the practice budget.
- A new learner gets prerequisite support; an experienced learner can use denser
  explanations after demonstrating the relevant prerequisite knowledge.
- Explain every correct answer and the misconception behind plausible distractors.
- Budget reading and thinking separately. Start with approximately 20 minutes
  reading and 10 minutes practice. Treat word-count estimates as rough; revise
  from reported elapsed time rather than padding or cutting useful explanation.
- Review source support, missing prerequisites, repeated content, assessment
  coverage, answer correctness, and tone before building.

The compiler checks structure and references. A passing build does not establish
factual correctness, suitable difficulty, enjoyable writing, or mastery. Those
require editorial review and observed learner responses.
